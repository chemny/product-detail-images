# Layout Archetypes And Blueprints

An archetype is not just a page name. Convert it into a concrete blueprint before generation.

## Required Blueprint Fields

For every page specify:

- subject region and approximate frame share;
- visual carrier: model, product, detail, concept, collage, card, diagram, or table;
- camera distance and subject state;
- scene mode: literal, reinterpret, or conceptual;
- 2-3 recognizable scene cues when copy names a concrete place or occasion;
- model action, expression, gaze, and camera angle when a person appears;
- text region and low-texture background area;
- headline width, line count, and alignment;
- Chinese-English size relationship;
- logo position;
- information density and label limit;
- allowed crop and required visible edges.

## Core Archetypes

### 1. Cinematic Hero

Full-bleed product or model scene with one dominant focal subject. Use one large positioning headline, one proof line, and restrained supporting text. Reserve one directed text zone rather than empty space on both sides.

### 2. Lifestyle Scene

Show a concrete use, wearing, or handling relationship. The action must reveal value; changing only the room or pose is insufficient.

### 3. Concept Visualization

Make the product visibly interact with light, water, air, botanicals, geometry, temperature, motion, craft, or another category-appropriate metaphor. The concept must form the composition, not appear as a decorative icon beside an ordinary product shot.

### 4. Single-Feature Close-Up

Let one visible feature dominate the page. Use one headline and one explanation. Avoid a full model, multiple complex callouts, and dense copy in the same frame.

### 5. Human-Worn Close-Up

Use the body relationship to explain fit, drape, neckline, scale, grip, or use. State the exact crop and which body or product edges must remain visible.

### 6. Dynamic Silhouette

Use walking, turning, folding, opening, pouring, handling, or another meaningful action. Movement must reveal silhouette or function.

### 7. Color Moodboard

Use an intentional collage or grid containing product crops, color references, inspiration objects, and color chips. Define cell proportions, gaps, alignment, and title zone. Keep inspiration distinct from unsupported material claims.

### 8. Styling Or Combination

Explain pairing, proportion, color coordination, kit relationship, or complementary use. Supporting items remain visually subordinate.

### 9. Specification File

Use a structured grid, table, diagram, or aligned cards. Only confirmed values appear as facts. Typography is horizontal and highly readable.

### 10. Review Or Trust

Use confirmed review, certification, award, process, origin, comparison, or brand evidence. If evidence is missing, replace the module rather than fabricating proof.

### 11. Care Or Usage Guide

Use short steps, icons, and clear hierarchy. Include only supplied or ordinary category-safe guidance; do not invent safety or warranty commitments.

## Commerce Layouts

Use these combinations when one page needs several related modules while retaining one visual center:

- **Hero plus advantage rail:** one dominant product image, one value headline, and 2-3 short advantage labels.
- **Main visual plus two proof crops:** one selling-point scene with two subordinate detail enlargements.
- **Scene plus decision cards:** one use image with compact occasion, audience, pairing, or selection information.
- **Detail evidence board:** one dominant structural detail plus two related supporting details and short explanations.
- **Moodboard plus buying guidance:** color or collection imagery combined with styling, occasion, or SKU guidance.
- **Product plus specification file:** one product view paired with confirmed parameters or a confirmed-information summary.
- **Visual peak composition:** one dominant product-advantage interaction using controlled scale, motion, or concept elements, with restrained proof text.
- **Evidence peak composition:** one decisive demonstration, comparison, capacity proof, structure view, or use relationship supported by concise annotations.
- **Information peak composition:** one clear mechanism, specification hierarchy, compatibility map, component system, or decision model with disciplined typography.
- **Product information index:** one complete product view plus a disciplined map of visible design, structure, use, styling, or confirmed facts. Explain the product directly; do not use preference checkboxes or survey-like questions.

One page may contain several related modules, but it must retain one primary commerce job and one primary visual center.

## Assignment Rules

- Avoid repeating an archetype in a six-image set unless the information carrier changes substantially.
- Larger sets may repeat only with a different visual carrier and blueprint.
- Use at least four visual carriers in a complete six-image set.
- Adjacent pages should differ in subject position, distance, state, and information density.
- Before generation, adjust adjacent pages that repeat the same setting, action, expression/gaze, distance, and angle so at least two dimensions differ. This is a soft storyboard check, not a post-generation pixel-matching rule.
- When copy names a concrete scene, show recognizable environmental evidence. If the intended image is abstract, rewrite the copy as conceptual rather than pretending the abstract space is a literal city, cafe, home, office, or outdoor setting.
- Keep one typography grid and brand world while varying page space and composition.
- If the prompt contains only an archetype name and adjectives, the blueprint is incomplete.

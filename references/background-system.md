# Background System

Use this system before planning visual directions or writing image prompts. Treat product restoration and environment restoration as separate decisions.

## Default Principle

Use uploaded material as the source of truth for the locked product, not as an automatic background template.

Default to replacing the source environment. Preserve or reinterpret it only when it provides necessary evidence, protected brand identity, meaningful scale/context, or an explicitly requested scene.

## Background Modes

Choose exactly one primary mode for the set and record the reason.

### Replace

Use by default when the environment is incidental to the product.

- Extract product-relevant invariants and design a new commercial environment from the product category, buyer, brand position, page role, and selected visual system.
- Do not carry over incidental architecture, surfaces, furniture, landscape, lighting, props, clutter, or spatial geometry.

### Reinterpret

Use when the environment type helps explain use, scale, fit, installation, or lifestyle context, but the literal source environment is not visually suitable.

- Preserve the environment's functional category, not its exact appearance.
- Rebuild materials, composition, lighting, cleanliness, and spatial hierarchy to match the selected commercial direction.

### Preserve

Use only when at least one condition is true:

- The user explicitly requests preservation.
- The environment is a protected brand asset or recognizable branded location.
- The scene is necessary product evidence, such as installed scale, real use, safety context, or environmental compatibility.
- Removing the environment would materially misrepresent the product.

Preservation still permits technical cleanup unless the user requests pixel-level fidelity.

## Background Selection Inputs

Choose the new background from these inputs, in priority order:

1. Product category and physical use.
2. Locked brand identity and price positioning.
3. Target buyer and platform.
4. Section role: hero, proof, detail, use, comparison, parameter, or summary.
5. Product color, reflectivity, transparency, texture, and silhouette needs.
6. Set-wide visual continuity.

Do not select a background merely because it appears in the uploaded image.

## Set-Wide Background Lock

Before generation, lock:

- Background mode: replace, reinterpret, or preserve.
- Environment language: studio, domestic, professional, outdoor, editorial, technical, natural, or another category-appropriate system.
- Material family: one coherent family of surfaces and structures.
- Palette and color temperature.
- Lighting direction, softness, and contrast.
- Perspective and horizon logic.
- Allowed scene variations by section.
- Source-environment elements explicitly forbidden from reappearing.

Sections may vary their setting while remaining inside the locked system. Do not mix unrelated material, lighting, architectural, or environmental languages in one set.

## One Brand World, Multiple Page Spaces

Consistency does not mean repeating one room or studio backdrop.

- Hero pages may use a complete emotional scene.
- Concept pages may use abstract metaphor spaces.
- Detail pages may use clean studio or close-up environments.
- Moodboards may use paper, grid, editorial, or object compositions.
- Specification and guide pages may use structured card systems.

Keep palette, color temperature, lighting character, typography, decorative language, and material quality coherent while varying the concrete space by page archetype.

## Prompt Requirements

The planning record keeps the selected background mode and rationale. The image-generation prompt needs only the actionable design:

- treat the source as product reference, not automatic environment reference;
- name source-environment elements that are likely to reappear and must be excluded;
- describe the new environment, materials, palette, lighting, spatial hierarchy, and physical support;
- keep the instruction positive and concrete rather than repeating the full background policy.

## Exceptions

- Do not remove required installation context, scale evidence, hazard context, or product-environment interaction.
- Do not invent branded locations, certifications, laboratories, clinics, factories, farms, or professional facilities as trust evidence.
- Do not use an aspirational environment to imply unsupported performance or suitability.
- Keep the product visually dominant and physically grounded in the new environment.

# Commerce Story And Poster Sequence

Plan the complete commerce story before choosing the image count. Image count controls how the story is compressed or expanded; it must not determine whether the detail page is complete.

## Mandatory Commerce Coverage

Every automatically planned detail page must cover:

1. **Product recognition:** what is being sold.
2. **Core value:** why the buyer should care.
3. **Advantage expression:** the strongest 1-3 reasons to choose it.
4. **Proof:** visible structure, detail, use, supplied information, or clearly conceptual support.
5. **Usage imagination:** how it looks, feels, fits, works, pairs, or belongs in the buyer's life.
6. **Purchase decision:** who it suits, how to choose, or what confirmed specification, SKU, care, package, trust, or caution information matters.

Before generation, name where each role appears. If any role is absent, the plan is incomplete.

## Automatic Count Tiers

Automatically choose only 6, 8, or 10 images.

- **6 images — minimum complete set:** use when one core value and a small number of visible advantages can be expressed without crowding.
- **8 images — default complete sales set:** use for most apparel, beauty, home, food, lifestyle, and consumer products; allows advantages, proof, imagination, and decision support to breathe.
- **10 images — rich commercial set:** use when the product has multiple SKUs, scenes, advantages, parameters, brand assets, trust evidence, or a longer purchase-decision path.

Choose the smallest tier that passes mandatory commerce coverage without overcrowding or generic filler. Material richness controls proof depth and may justify expansion. Image clarity affects restoration precision, never count.

Do not automatically generate four images. If the user explicitly requests four, label the deliverable `compact_showcase`, explain the compressed or omitted decision content, and do not present it as the default complete-detail-page tier.

## Value Tree Before Sequence

Organize analysis into:

- one core value;
- 1-3 advantage pillars;
- visible or supplied evidence for each pillar;
- usage or ownership imagination;
- purchase-decision information and likely buyer concerns.

Five candidate selling points remain useful inputs, but do not treat them as five equal pages.

## Stable Skeletons

### 6 Images — Minimum Complete Detail Page

1. **Positioning Overview:** product recognition, core value, and 2-3 short advantage labels.
2. **Buyer Scene Or Usage Imagination:** show the need, occasion, styling, or use relationship.
3. **Core Advantage Overview:** present the strongest advantage combination through one dominant visual idea.
4. **Structure And Detail Proof:** one main proof plus up to two related supporting details.
5. **Second Advantage / Dynamic / Combination:** deepen another reason to choose or show value in action.
6. **Purchase Decision:** color, SKU, specification, audience, pairing, care, package, trust, or confirmed-information summary.

### 8 Images — Default Complete Sales Narrative

1. Hero positioning and core value.
2. Buyer need, lifestyle, or use scene.
3. Core advantage overview.
4. Advantage pillar 01.
5. Advantage pillar 02.
6. Detail, structure, dynamic, or use proof.
7. Color, styling, brand, collection, comparison, or trust expression.
8. Specification, selection, care, usage, package, or purchase guide.

Designate one of pages 3-6 as the `series_peak_page`. Choose `visual_peak`, `evidence_peak`, or `information_peak` from category, buyer needs, available facts, and decision complexity.

### 10 Images — Rich Commercial Narrative

1. Hero positioning.
2. Buyer need or use scene.
3. Core advantage overview.
4. Advantage pillar 01.
5. Advantage pillar 02.
6. Advantage pillar 03 or additional proof.
7. Usage, styling, comparison, review, detail, or trust proof.
8. Brand, color, collection, origin, or confidence layer.
9. Specification, parameter, size, SKU, or confirmed product-information page.
10. Usage, care, selection, package, after-sales, caution, or trust summary.

Designate at least one page between 3-7 as the `series_peak_page`.

## Page Composition Principle

Each page has one primary commerce job and one primary visual center, but may contain several related modules. A hero may include a value headline and three advantage labels; a detail page may include one main detail and two supporting crops; a decision page may combine color, occasion, and pairing guidance.

Reject both extremes:

- a beautiful page with no new commerce value;
- an overloaded page combining unrelated brand, specification, usage, and selling-point messages.

## Series Peak Rule

Six-image sets should contain a clear high point when the category supports it. Eight- and ten-image sets must designate one peak type:

- **Visual peak:** image-led fashion, beauty, food, lifestyle, gift, or other desire-led products. Use a memorable concept, motion system, scale change, or product-linked metaphor.
- **Evidence peak:** bags, home goods, tools, appliances, or products whose choice depends on structure, capacity, durability evidence, comparison, or use demonstration.
- **Information peak:** electronics, professional equipment, complex kits, or high-consideration products whose strongest moment is a clear mechanism, specification hierarchy, compatibility map, or decision model.

Every peak must:

- express the core value or strongest advantage;
- keep the sold product recognizable and structurally faithful;
- answer a purchase question when the headline is hidden;
- provide information, proof, or desire that another page does not already provide.

A generic portrait, abstract background, unsupported data dashboard, or mood-only image is not a valid peak.

## Final-Page Novelty Rule

The final decision page must add information not already delivered by the SKU and styling pages. Prefer:

- confirmed specification or product information;
- product information index or visible design overview;
- package, care, usage, compatibility, or caution guidance;
- visible-feature map that explains the product rather than testing the buyer;
- buyer concern summary.

Do not use the final page merely to repeat color names, occasion labels, or earlier selling points.
Do not turn the final page into a preference survey. Avoid checkbox or questionnaire copy such as `你是否`, `你喜欢`, `你偏爱`, or `你在意`. When objective specifications are unavailable, summarize the product's visible design, structure, use, or styling information instead of interrogating the buyer.

## Replacement Rule

Keep commerce coverage intact when information is missing:

- unsupported objective proof → visible product proof;
- unavailable review → comparison, detail, process, or another trust carrier;
- missing specification → confirmed information at a glance;
- missing care data → styling, usage, package, selection, or audience guidance;
- unsupported technical performance → clearly conceptual aesthetic or sensory expression.

Do not invent objective facts and do not replace a decision role with generic mood imagery.

## Visual Variation

For every page lock the commerce role, visual carrier, subject state, camera distance, subject position, layout blueprint, and background space.

For complete sets:

- use at least three camera distances and three subject states;
- use at least four visual carriers in a six-image set;
- avoid repeating the same pose/state on adjacent pages;
- do not place adjacent subjects in the same horizontal zone;
- do not rely on copy or background changes alone for variation;
- cover the text mentally: pages must remain visually distinct;
- maintain one palette, typography system, lighting character, and brand world.

## Format Defaults

- Marketplace detail sections: 9:16.
- Xiaohongshu cards: 3:4 or 4:5 with lower text density.
- Douyin shop or cover: 9:16 with stronger first-message hierarchy.
- Single long detail image: only when explicitly requested.
